# No existing tool reads the lab's own inventory

Across four tool families surveyed in October 2026 (FAIR assessors, data-management-plan engines, ELN/LIMS and offline validators, and knowledge-graph intake frameworks), **no tool starts from the project list a wet lab already keeps, checks the files on its server against that list, and returns an owned, evidenced action plan per project.** FAIR assessors such as F-UJI and FAIR-Checker take a URL or DOI and inspect web metadata, so they work only after deposit ([F-UJI README](https://github.com/pangaea-data-publisher/fuji); [FAIR-Checker paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13326-023-00289-5)). DMP engines such as DSW, DMPTool and ARGOS take questionnaire answers and emit a plan document; none reads files, and their makers acknowledge that follow-up against actual practice is rare ([DSW overview](https://guide.ds-wizard.org/en/4.34/about/introduction/overview.html); [Tampere IDCC26 poster](https://zenodo.org/records/18793447)). ELNs link samples to raw files only when the lab has already adopted that ELN and registered the file through it, and the thin survey evidence says most academic faculty use no ELN ([Harvard FASRC report](https://www.rc.fas.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/DISSEMINATION-COPY_-RSpace-Support-Group-Summary-Report.pdf)). Knowledge-graph projects (Biolink, BioCypher, OptimusKG, HuBMAP, CELLxGENE) validate at or after intake and expect per-source and per-edge provenance that a lab must prepare itself ([Biolink primary_knowledge_source](https://biolink.github.io/biolink-model/primary_knowledge_source/)). The one framework that touches local files, FAIRSCAPE, packages computational workflow outputs into RO-Crates rather than wet-lab sample sheets ([FAIRSCAPE preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.23.629818v1.full)). LemonAId, a local, single-file, inventory-first readiness planner with sample-sheet and FCS-header readers and a Biolink-aligned graph export, therefore occupies an unoccupied middle: upstream of the FAIR assessors, downstream of the DMP, independent of any ELN, and feeding the graph. Its value comes from not duplicating any of these layers and instead adopting their rubrics (RDA FDMM levels, Bridge2AI 0/1 sub-criteria), their formats (maDMP JSON, RO-Crate, Frictionless, `.eln`) and their identifier conventions (infores, ORCID, ROR, RRID, Biolink CURIEs) so its outputs flow into them.

## FAIR assessors verify published records, so they belong after LemonAId, not inside it

Every automated FAIR assessor in current use inspects metadata exposed on the web. F-UJI's controller is `assess_by_id`; it harvests metadata from the landing page, PID providers such as DataCite, and services such as re3data, and its own README states that it tests only the surface of interoperability and reuse and that results depend on the FAIR-enabling services around the object ([F-UJI README](https://github.com/pangaea-data-publisher/fuji)). It is actively maintained (v3.5.1 released 14 October 2025, with a v4.0.0 citation dated 2026), MIT licensed, self-hostable with pip or Docker, and since v3.5.0 it emits W3C DQV RDF alongside JSON ([Zenodo 17350027](https://zenodo.org/records/17350027); [F-UJI releases](https://github.com/pangaea-data-publisher/fuji/releases)). FAIR-Checker takes a web page URL, extracts embedded JSON-LD, RDFa or microdata and tests it with SPARQL and SHACL across 11 metrics, returning per-metric pass or fail plus "must be" and "should be" recommendations linked to FAIR Cookbook recipes ([J Biomed Semantics 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13326-023-00289-5)). The FAIR Evaluator takes a single GUID and its own paper warns that "Passing all of these tests does not indicate that 'you are FAIR'" ([Sci Data 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-019-0184-5)). FAIRshake hosts community rubrics for manual or automated scoring and renders an insignia, but we found no 2024 to 2026 activity signal on its pages ([FAIRshake GitHub](https://github.com/MaayanLab/FAIRshake); [PubMed 31677972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31677972/)). FAIR-Aware is a 19-question self-assessment of a researcher's understanding and is "not aimed at providing a score" for a dataset ([FAIRsFAIR 2020 PDF](https://www.fairsfair.eu/sites/default/files/20200701%20FAIRsFAIR%20Launches%20FAIR%20Assessment%20Tool%20.pdf)).

For a group whose flow, sequencing and imaging data still sit on a lab server, these tools are post-deposit verifiers. LemonAId should not re-implement any FAIR metric test. What it can take from this family is a scale and a remediation library. The RDA FAIR Data Maturity Model defines **41 indicators** (Findable 7, Accessible 12, Interoperable 12, Reusable 10), prioritised Essential 20, Important 14, Useful 7, and scores each on levels 0 to 4 where levels 1 to 3 ("not being considered yet", "under consideration or planning", "in implementation") explicitly describe data that is not yet published ([RDA FDMM v0.90](https://archive.rd-alliance.org/system/files/FAIR%20Data%20Maturity%20Model_%20specification%20and%20guidelines_v0.90.pdf)). The model's authors wrote it for "owners of FAIR assessment methodologies, such as questionnaires and automated tools" and left the "how" to implementers, which is an invitation for a planner to adopt the scale as its per-project status vocabulary (inference). The FAIR Cookbook already functions as the remediation layer for FAIR-Checker, so a LemonAId action item can link to a Cookbook recipe the same way ([FAIR Cookbook home](https://faircookbook.elixir-europe.org/content/home.html)). The synergy runs forward as well: once a project earns a GEO, SRA or Zenodo record, LemonAId can attach the F-UJI JSON or DQV report to that project's "Findable" item as closing evidence, which turns a one-time score into a plan milestone (inference).

The NIH Bridge2AI criteria are the rubric with the most direct bearing on an AI-readiness planner. Bridge2AI defines AI-readiness as a set of characteristics of a dataset and its metadata that allow reliable, ethical AI analysis for defined use cases, states that "AI-readiness therefore implies that data must be FAIR", and operationalises it as **seven criteria (FAIR, Provenance, Characterization, Pre-model Explainability, Ethics, Sustainability, Computability) with about 28 sub-criteria each scored 0 or 1** and reported as a percentage per criterion; the authors add that readiness "is a dynamic property of specific data sets. It is context-dependent and developmental" and applies during data generation, not only at deposit ([Bridge2AI preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.23.619844v1.full)). A binary sub-criterion with an evidence attachment is almost exactly a planner line item, and the "per use case" clause justifies scoring readiness against a named downstream use such as a center knowledge graph rather than against a universal bar (inference). Two cautions: the criteria "require substantial metadata beyond what a DataCite DOI registration needs", so a Zenodo DOI alone will not satisfy them, and NIH ODSS itself has issued no formal checklist, saying only that "Making data AI/ML-ready is not simply formulaic" ([NOT-OD-23-082](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-23-082.html)).

FAIRSCAPE is the closest existing analogue to a local file-level tool and the one most worth interoperating with rather than reinventing. Its `fairscape-cli` builds RO-Crates incrementally on the user's machine, generates JSON Schema for tabular and HDF5 files and validates them with Frictionless, records provenance with the Evidence Graph Ontology, and "currently supports twenty-two of the twenty-eight AI-readiness criteria recommended by the NIH Bridge2AI Program"; it is MIT licensed with Zenodo releases in January and February 2026 ([FAIRSCAPE preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.23.629818v1.full); [Zenodo 18234493](https://zenodo.org/records/18234493)). Its importers are Snakemake, Cromwell, Galaxy, MLflow, REDCap, Frictionless and Croissant, and its outputs are `ro-crate-metadata.json`, HTML datasheets, evidence graphs, Croissant, PROV and LinkML schemas ([fairscape.github.io](https://fairscape.github.io/)). That import list shows the difference in starting point: FAIRSCAPE begins where a pipeline ends, whereas LemonAId begins with the ordering sheet, the sample sheet and the FCS header. The natural division is that LemonAId inventories, scores and plans, then hands a project that is ready for packaging to FAIRSCAPE or emits a minimal `ro-crate-metadata.json` that FAIRSCAPE's grader can read (inference).

## DMP engines plan from questionnaires and never look back at the data

The four planning engines share one input: human answers to templated questions. DSW organises knowledge models as branching questionnaire trees and renders one set of answers into PDF, Word or machine-actionable RDF and JSON, assigning a UUID to every question and answer ([DSW overview](https://guide.ds-wizard.org/en/4.34/about/introduction/overview.html); [DSW machine-actionability](https://ds-wizard.org/machine-actionability)). It is Apache-2.0, part of the ELIXIR infrastructure, at version 4.35, and institutions do customise its knowledge models, as ELIXIR UK documented in 2025 ([github.com/ds-wizard](https://github.com/ds-wizard); [ELIXIR UK blog](https://elixiruknode.org/blog/2025/customising-data-stewardship-wizard-for-an-institute/)). DMPTool, run by a "small team" at the California Digital Library, was still awaiting its rebuilt release in February 2026; its roadmap items are an LLM drafting assistant ("DMP Chef"), an API following a common RDA standard, and matching of published DOIs to a registered DMP ID ([UC3 blog, 2 Feb 2026](https://uc3.cdlib.org/?p=4759)). DMP Roadmap, the MIT-licensed Rails code behind DMPonline and DMPTool, says nothing about maDMP in its README ([DMPRoadmap/roadmap](https://github.com/DMPRoadmap/roadmap)). ARGOS, built on the EUPL-licensed OpenCDMP platform, added in September 2025 a Plan Evaluator that checks a plan against the RDA maDMP standard, FAIR principles and OSTrails benchmarks with pass or fail results and a timestamped history, but the page is explicit that it assesses "if datasets described are FAIR", meaning the description text, not the data ([ARGOS Q3 2025 release](https://www.openaire.eu/argos-q3-2025-release-big-updates-and-a-new-look); [OpenCDMP GitHub](https://github.com/OpenCDMP/OpenCDMP)).

The guidance layer is equally questionnaire-shaped. RDMkit, with **218 contributors, 134 guidelines and 673 referenced tools**, has no method for assessing a lab's own holdings; its "Existing data" page treats the question as finding other people's data to reuse and points only to DSW's "Is there any pre-existing data?" question ([RDMkit paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12485516/); [RDMkit Existing data](https://rdmkit.elixir-europe.org/existing_data)). The FAIRplus FAIRification Process is the one framework that prescribes tasks "with resources, time estimates, and owners" in roughly three-month sprints after a GO or NO GO review, but it does so as a human project-management method on a document template, not as tool output ([FAIR Cookbook FAIRification process](https://faircookbook.elixir-europe.org/content/recipes/introduction/fairification-process.html)). Its Dataset Maturity Model assesses real datasets through "a dedicated manual assessment template" applied by trained raters, mostly retrospectively across 20 projects ([FAIR Cookbook DSM](https://faircookbook.elixir-europe.org/content/recipes/maturity.html)).

The gap between the plan and the data two years later is acknowledged by the field. A Tampere University study of DMPs submitted to the Research Council of Finland in 2021 to 2022 found that sharing outcomes "often remain unclear" and concluded that DMP tools need better support for tracking and verification ([Zenodo 18793447](https://zenodo.org/records/18793447)). The only post-award mechanisms in 2026 are DMPTool's DOI matching and ARGOS's re-evaluation of plan text; nothing compares a plan to the lab's actual storage. This is the seam LemonAId occupies: an inventory re-run each year, diffed against the proposal-time plan (inference).

The format bridge already exists and LemonAId should use it rather than invent one. The RDA DMP Common Standard requires at root `contact`, `created`, `dataset` (one or more), `dmp_id`, `ethical_issues_exist`, `language`, `modified` and `title`; each `dataset` requires `dataset_id`, `personal_data`, `sensitive_data` and `title`, with optional `distribution` (format, byte size, `host` with storage type and backup frequency), `metadata` (standard identifiers such as MIFlowCyt or MINSEQE), `technical_resource` (instruments) and `contributor` (name plus role) ([RDA-DMP-Common-Standard](https://github.com/RDA-DMP-Common/RDA-DMP-Common-Standard)). Each LemonAId inventory entry maps onto one `dataset` with those slots. What does not fit is readiness itself: the standard has no status vocabulary, "unknown" is allowed only for four fields, and extension fields are permitted but "do not guarantee interoperability" ([RDA-DMP-Common-Standard](https://github.com/RDA-DMP-Common/RDA-DMP-Common-Standard)). The cleanest design is a strict maDMP export plus a readiness sidecar keyed by `dataset_id`, which keeps the plan consumable by ARGOS (which documents maDMP import), by the forthcoming DMPTool API, and by DSW templates, while the sidecar carries owners, missing fields and evidence (inference; [ARGOS maDMP import](https://zenodo.org/records/3944314/files/Import%20and%20Export%20of%20maDMPs%20from%20Argos_OpenDMP.pdf)).

## ELNs link samples to files only after adoption, and validators check one format each

Sample-to-file traceability exists as a data-model feature in several systems, always on the condition that the file entered through the system. openBIS attaches datasets to samples or experiments as a first-class relationship, offers a watched-folder dropbox that registers files to samples, and is on an active release train (20.10.12 in August 2025 through 7.0.0 on 2 July 2026, which added an experimental RO-Crate server) ([openBIS changelog](https://openbis.readthedocs.io/en/7.x/system-documentation/changelog/CHANGELOG.html)). RSpace links Inventory samples to notebook records through Lists of Materials with persistent global IDs, can link files held in institutional filestores rather than copying them, and exports RO-Crate; its code is AGPL-3.0 ([RSpace features](https://www.researchspace.com/features); [rspace-web GitHub](https://github.com/rspace-os/rspace-web)). eLabFTW (AGPL-3.0) links Experiments to Resources and imports or exports `.eln` archives and CSV ([eLabFTW GitHub](https://github.com/elabftw/elabftw); [eLabFTW import/export](https://doc.elabftw.net/import-export.html)). FAIRDOM-SEEK carries the ISA structure (sample, assay, data file) and exports ISA-JSON when an admin enables it ([SEEK user guide](https://docs.seek4science.org/help/user-guide/)). Benchling's free academic tier excludes Inventory and Registry, which were "built for industry requirements", so academic Benchling users cannot build the link structurally ([Benchling for Academics](https://benchling.com/academic)). None of these scans an existing directory tree against a sample sheet.

Whether labs fill these links in is the harder question, and the evidence says rarely. Harvard's RSpace pilot surveyed 754 people, received **15 complete responses, one self-reported active RSpace user, and found that "most faculty used no ELN"** ([Harvard FASRC report](https://www.rc.fas.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/DISSEMINATION-COPY_-RSpace-Support-Group-Summary-Report.pdf)). A 2022 UK physical-sciences survey (n=44) found paper still dominant, electronic data linked by "hyperlinks, file paths, dates, codes, and unique IDs", and researchers who "often fail to capture useful metadata" ([Kanza et al. PSDI](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/476747/1/PSDI_Paper_Accepted_Manuscript.pdf)). A vendor-sponsored 2025 industry survey (n=150) reported that 65% had repeated experiments because earlier results were hard to find, a figure to cite with its industry and vendor caveats ([Business Wire, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260127524632/en)). No published survey quantifies sample-to-file link completeness in academic ELNs; that is a measurement gap LemonAId's own inventories could begin to fill (inference).

Reagent provenance is thinner still. Lot tracking is documented for Quartzy, Labguru and LabArchives Inventory, but an independent comparison reports "Not stated" for catalog numbers, antibody fields and RRID across Quartzy, Labguru, SciNote and LabArchives ([IntuitionLabs comparison](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/quartzy-labguru-scinote-labarchives-comparison)). No tool found reads a purchasing or ordering spreadsheet. The Antibody Registry, however, exposes a public JSON API (`GET /fts-antibodies?q=...`, `GET /antibodies/{id}`) returning `rrid`, `cloneId`, `vendorName`, `catalogNum` and `catAlt` without authentication ([ToolUniverse source](https://zitniklab.hms.harvard.edu/ToolUniverse/_modules/tooluniverse/antibody_registry_tool.html)). An ordering sheet already carries vendor and catalog number, which are the join keys the Registry needs, so a planner that ingests the sheet can produce an RRID-annotated reagent list before the lab adopts any inventory product; catalog number is not a filterable parameter, so matching happens client-side on the returned fields (inference from the API description).

The offline validators are building blocks LemonAId should call or emulate, not rewrite. `isatools` validates ISA-Tab or ISA-JSON locally with a bundled default configuration and returns a JSON report of errors and warnings ([isa-api validation](https://isa-api.readthedocs.io/en/latest/validation.html)). Frictionless validates a local CSV or `datapackage.json` and emits JSON or YAML metadata ([Frictionless console](https://framework.frictionlessdata.io/docs/console/overview.html)). `cellxgene-schema` 7.1.2 (MIT, 14 October 2026) validates a local h5ad against the CELLxGENE schema from the command line ([PyPI cellxgene-schema](https://pypi.org/project/cellxgene-schema); [single-cell-curation](https://github.com/chanzuckerberg/single-cell-curation)). CEDAR templates are JSON Schema and instances "can be checked inside or outside CEDAR" ([CEDAR template overview](https://metadatacenter.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user-guide/sections/c1/1_template_overview/)). ENA and MIxS checklists are downloadable XML, though official validation happens server-side at Webin submission ([ENA programmatic samples](https://ena-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/submit/samples/programmatic.html)). For flow cytometry specifically, MIFlowCyt remains a 2008 checklist document with no maintained validator, and FlowRepository has posted a suspension of new experiment creation because it is "continuously running out of space" ([MIFlowCyt 2008 PDF](https://flowcore.hsc.wvu.edu/media/15842/miflowcyt-std-2008.pdf); [FlowRepository quick start](https://flowrepository.org/quick_start_guide)). That leaves LemonAId's FCS-header reader as the only practical MIFlowCyt check in a lab's hands in 2026, with the caveat that header keywords cover only part of the checklist and experiment-level fields must come from the sample sheet (inference).

The packaging target for an ELN-independent planner is the `.eln` format, a ZIP whose root is an RO-Crate, imported and exported by eLabFTW, RSpace, Kadi4Mat, SampleDB and others, with LabCollector adding support in August 2026 ([RO-Crate ELN page](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/eln); [LabCollector press release](https://lp.labcollector.com/press/labcollector-introduces-eln-consortium-import-export)). A LemonAId project that reaches a defined readiness level can be exported as `.eln` so a lab that later adopts eLabFTW or RSpace imports the inventory rather than retyping it (inference).

## Knowledge-graph intake expects per-edge provenance that labs do not yet produce

Biolink puts provenance on the edge. Each association carries exactly one `primary_knowledge_source` (an infores CURIE, "Only one data source should be declared primary"), an optional list of `aggregator_knowledge_source`, a `knowledge_level` drawn from seven values (`knowledge_assertion`, `logical_entailment`, `prediction`, `statistical_association`, `text_co_occurrence`, `observation`, `not_provided`) and an `agent_type` drawn from eight (`manual_agent`, `automated_agent`, `data_analysis_pipeline`, `computational_model`, `text_mining_agent`, `image_processing_agent`, `manual_validation_of_automated_agent`, `not_provided`) ([primary_knowledge_source](https://biolink.github.io/biolink-model/primary_knowledge_source/); [KnowledgeLevelEnum](https://biolink.github.io/biolink-model/KnowledgeLevelEnum); [AgentTypeEnum](https://biolink.github.io/biolink-model/AgentTypeEnum)). The Reference Ingest Guide schema records `knowledge_level` and `agent_type` per edge type per source, along with licence, terms of use and source status ([Biolink RIG schema](https://biolink.github.io/resource-ingest-guide-schema/)). BioCypher, the adapter framework, treats `schema_config.yaml` as the single source of truth, expects CURIE-like node IDs, and in strict mode requires `source`, `version` and `licence` as provenance fields, at adapter rather than edge granularity ([BioCypher adapter docs](https://biocypher.github.io/biocypher/latest/llms-adapters.txt)). OptimusKG, built on Biolink 3.2.1 through BioCypher, carries `sources` with `direct` and `indirect` lists on every node and edge, flags `relation_conflict`, and enforces that every node ID is a Biolink-compliant CURIE; its code is MIT and its data CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([OptimusKG arXiv 2604.27269](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27269)).

For LemonAId's Biolink-aligned export, the practical minimum a lab must prepare is an infores-style identifier for the lab dataset, a licence, a version or date, per-edge-type declarations of `knowledge_level` and `agent_type` (for example `statistical_association` plus `data_analysis_pipeline` for a differential-expression edge), and CURIE prefixes from Biolink-allowed namespaces (inference from the slot definitions). We found **no published mapping from Biolink `knowledge_level` or `agent_type` to W3C PROV-O**; the natural alignment of `agent_type` to the class of `prov:Agent`, `primary_knowledge_source` to `prov:hadPrimarySource` and the aggregator chain to `prov:wasDerivedFrom` is our inference, and `knowledge_level` has no PROV counterpart because PROV describes derivation rather than epistemic status ([W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)). LemonAId should state its own mapping explicitly rather than claim a standard one.

Intake validation sits mostly on the receiving side. Of the commons reviewed, only CELLxGENE and HuBMAP ship a validator a lab can run locally against the exact schema the receiver enforces. HuBMAP's `ingest-validation-tools` (MIT) checks that an upload directory holds `*-metadata.tsv` files whose rows correspond one-to-one with dataset directories, with `contributors.tsv` and `antibodies.tsv` as required companions, while field-level checks run in the hosted CEDAR Metadata Spreadsheet Validator ([ingest-validation-tools](https://github.com/hubmapconsortium/ingest-validation-tools); [HuBMAP Section 3](https://docs.hubmapconsortium.org/data-submission/Section3)). That split, structure locally and vocabulary in a hosted service, is a workable pattern for a single-file planner that must stay offline (inference). Gen3 validates TSVs server-side and returns per-record errors such as enumerated-value violations and `INVALID_LINK`; a community `gen3-validator` (2.3.0, August 2026) adds a lab-side check ([Gen3 user guide](https://uc-cdis.github.io/gen3-user-doc/user-guide/data-contribution); [PyPI gen3-validator](https://pypi.org/project/gen3-validator/)). NCI GDC and NHLBI BioData Catalyst validate after upload, and BDC's requirements are dominated by governance and file hygiene: dbGaP registration, consent-group buckets, separate data dictionaries per file, lowercase extensions and no spaces in names ([GDC checklist](https://docs.gdc.cancer.gov/Data_Submission_Portal/Users_Guide/Checklist); [BDC submission instructions](https://bdcatalyst.gitbook.io/biodata-catalyst-documentation/data-management/data-submission-instructions)).

The ontology set a stem-cell or single-cell center must hit is well defined because CELLxGENE enforces it with a local validator: NCBITaxon for organism, CL for cell type, UBERON for tissue, MONDO or PATO:0000461 for disease or normal, EFO for assay, HsapDv or MmusDv for development stage, PATO for sex, HANCESTRO for ethnicity, Ensembl gene IDs in `var`, and raw counts required ([CELLxGENE Contribute](https://cellxgene.cziscience.com/docs/032__Contribute%20and%20Publish%20Data)). HCA Tier 1 fields mirror this as "a prerequisite for atlas building" ([HCA metadata](https://data.humancellatlas.org/metadata)). By contrast the Bridge2AI Standards Working Group, whose four Grand Challenges lean clinical and voice, recommends Datasheets for Datasets in LinkML, ORCID for PIs, ROR for institutions, PIDs per dataset version, Croissant and RO-Crate for machine-readable description, and Frictionless or JSON Schema data dictionaries, and names no life-science ontology ([arXiv 2509.10432](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10432v2)). A readiness planner for cell biology has to layer the CELLxGENE set on top of the Bridge2AI identifiers (inference).

RO-Crate is the packaging layer that joins all of this. RO-Crate 1.3 is the current long-term release under Apache 2.0; its provenance model records `CreateAction` and `UpdateAction` with ORCID-identified agents, software or equipment as `instrument` (equipment "SHOULD have a serial number and manufacturer"), and ISO 8601 timestamps ([RO-Crate specification](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification.html); [RO-Crate 1.2 provenance](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.2/provenance.html)). The Workflow Run Crate profiles "are mapped to W3C PROV-O using SKOS" ([arXiv 2312.07852](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07852v2)). Bridge2AI CM4AI packages datasets as RO-Crates with schema.org, DataCite, EVI and Frictionless schemas and resolves them with ARK identifiers ([arXiv 2509.10432](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10432v2)). No project reviewed converts RO-Crate manifests directly into Biolink edges; the join point is identifiers (ARK, DOI, ORCID, ROR), not a shared graph schema. One instrument-level record in an RO-Crate (cytometer serial number, acquisition software version, operator ORCID) would satisfy both the Bridge2AI provenance criterion and the Biolink `agent_type` declaration for edges derived from that run (inference).

No knowledge-graph project publishes a before-and-after answerability baseline. OptimusKG reports 190,939 nodes and 21,818,752 edges and validates by literature support for sampled edges (70.0% of true, 16.6% of false), not by counting questions answerable before the graph existed ([OptimusKG arXiv 2604.27269](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27269)). A "readiness query" baseline computed from a center's inventories before graph construction would be new relative to documented practice (inference).

## Where LemonAId sits: the unoccupied middle and the synergy map

The gap is specific. Upstream tools plan from templates without an inventory behind them. Downstream tools score records that already have a DOI, or validate files that already sit inside a commons or an ELN. Between them, nothing reads the project list and the folders a wet lab actually has, scores each project against a named downstream use, assigns an owner and an evidence requirement to each missing item, and re-runs a year later. Every source surveyed is consistent with that absence, though a search of this breadth cannot prove a negative, and commercial readiness consultancies were out of scope.

LemonAId's design choices map onto that gap. Local and single-file means it runs where the data is, with no server and no account, which matters because the FAIR assessors need a web-exposed record and the DMP engines need a hosted instance. Inventory-first means the lab's existing spreadsheet is the input, not a questionnaire. Evidence-based means each item closes with an artifact (a validator report, a DOI, a signed-off sample sheet) rather than a self-report, which distinguishes it from FAIR-Aware and FAIRshake's manual mode. Sample-sheet and FCS-header readers give it the modality knowledge the DMP engines lack and the validators only have for their own formats. The Biolink-aligned export with provenance points it at a graph intake that BioCypher and OptimusKG define precisely. A project premise worth stating in the README is that the graph takes processed ("gold") data, not raw files, which matches OptimusKG's medallion pipeline and keeps raw FASTQ and FCS out of the graph export.

What LemonAId should not duplicate: FAIR metric tests (F-UJI, FAIR-Checker), DMP authoring and funder templates (DSW, DMPTool, ARGOS), notebook and inventory systems (eLabFTW, RSpace, openBIS), format validators (isatools, Frictionless, cellxgene-schema), RO-Crate packaging and grading (FAIRSCAPE), and graph construction (BioCypher, OptimusKG). What it should adopt: the RDA FDMM level scale, Bridge2AI's seven criteria and binary sub-criteria, maDMP JSON as the plan export, RO-Crate and `.eln` as the package export, Frictionless Table Schema for sample sheets, CELLxGENE's required fields as the single-cell minimum, HuBMAP's `antibodies.tsv` and `contributors.tsv` conventions for reagents and people, infores plus ORCID plus ROR plus RRID as identifiers, and Biolink's `knowledge_level` and `agent_type` on every exported edge.

## Honest limits of this search

Several facts that readers may want could not be verified from primary sources in this run. GitHub API access and commit pages were blocked, so last-commit dates for F-UJI, FAIR-Checker, FAIRshake, FAIR Evaluator, RSpace and SciNote rest on release pages, Zenodo records and version strings; F-UJI v4.0.0's release date and any licence change after it are unknown, and FAIRshake's licence and 2024 to 2026 activity remain unconfirmed. FAIR-Aware's current status sits behind a bot check, and the FAIR Evaluator's "offline for a short time" notice is undated. Two DSW GitHub pages disagreed about whether `engine-backend` was archived in September 2026; the project itself shows August 2026 commits and a 4.35 release, so we treat the archive notice as a possible repository consolidation rather than abandonment. DMPonline's own site, its subscription model and any maDMP API were not fetched, and whether DSW can import maDMP JSON in 2026 is unverified. The full text of Bridge2AI Table 1 (the roughly 28 sub-criteria) could not be retrieved because PMC was behind reCAPTCHA and the bioRxiv rendering showed an empty table; anyone quoting sub-criteria should pull them from PMC11526931 or the bioRxiv PDF first. Whether `cellxgene-schema` and HuBMAP's validators run fully offline is not stated on their pages. We did not confirm native RRID or clone fields in any commercial inventory product, the Antibody Registry's rate limits, SEEK's ISA-JSON export contents, or LabArchives and Labfolder sample-to-file linking from primary documentation. The Translator Question-of-the-Month paper and the later sections of the OptimusKG preprint were not read, so a no-graph baseline there cannot be ruled out. No peer-reviewed 2020 to 2026 survey quantifies ELN adoption or metadata completeness in academic biology labs; the Harvard, Southampton and Sapio figures are the best available and each carries a sample or sponsorship caveat. Commercial data-readiness consultancies and vendor AI-readiness scorecards were out of scope. Finally, the central claim that no tool is inventory-first is an absence-of-evidence finding across roughly 150 fetched sources, not a proof.

## Conclusion

The 2026 landscape has matured at both ends and stayed empty in the middle. Assessment rubrics (RDA FDMM, Bridge2AI), plan formats (maDMP), package formats (RO-Crate, `.eln`), file validators (Frictionless, isatools, cellxgene-schema) and graph schemas (Biolink, BioCypher) are each stable enough to build on, and a readiness planner that invents its own versions of any of them would weaken rather than strengthen its position. The work that remains undone is the join: reading what a lab already keeps, scoring it against a named use, and carrying owners and evidence through to a package and a graph export. That join is also where the field's documented failures live, in the DMP that nobody revisits, the ELN that most faculty never adopt, and the provenance fields a graph needs but a bench scientist has never been asked for.

Two implications follow for the README and the paper. First, LemonAId's claim should be positional, not comparative: it is the pre-deposit, pre-ELN, pre-graph step that none of the listed tools performs, and its outputs are designed to be consumed by them. Second, because no published baseline measures how much of a center's data could answer a question before a graph existed, and no survey measures sample-to-file completeness in academic labs, LemonAId's inventories can produce those numbers as a by-product. That measurement, repeated across labs, would be a contribution in its own right, independent of the software.
